Introduction

Buying your first bitcoin is easier than most people think. This short guide walks you through the process safely, with a focus on beginners in Canada. It is the personal starting point; if you are thinking about bitcoin for a business balance sheet, see Bitcoin for businesses.

Important: Bitcoin is volatile and involves risk. Only use money you can afford to lose. This is general education, not financial advice. Do your own research.

Step 1: Understand the basics

  • Bitcoin is decentralized digital money. No bank or government controls it.
  • There will only ever be 21 million bitcoin. It is scarce by design.
  • You don’t need to buy a whole coin. You can buy any amount (even $10 worth).
  • The smallest unit is called a “satoshi” (sat). 1 BTC = 100,000,000 sats.

Want more background first? Read what Bitcoin is.

Step 2: Set up a secure wallet

A wallet lets you control your bitcoin. “Not your keys, not your coins.”

For small amounts (under $500)

Use the wallet built into Shakepay or another reputable mobile app to start.

For larger amounts

Get a hardware wallet (Ledger, Trezor, or Coldcard). These keep your keys offline and are much safer.

Critical: When you set up a wallet, you will receive a 12- or 24-word seed phrase. Write it down on paper. Never photograph it, store it on your phone, or share it with anyone — including us. Curtis Bitcoin Company will never ask for your seed phrase.

Step 3: Choose a Canadian platform

For most beginners in Canada, Shakepay is a simple option:

Step 4: Buy your first bitcoin

  1. Sign up on Shakepay (or your chosen platform) and complete identity verification (required by Canadian law).
  2. Deposit CAD using Interac e-Transfer (usually arrives in minutes).
  3. Buy bitcoin. Start small — $50 or $100 is plenty for a first purchase.
  4. Download your transaction history. You will want it at tax time.

Tip: Many people use “dollar-cost averaging” — buying a fixed small amount regularly instead of trying to time the market.

Step 5: Secure your bitcoin

Once you hold more than a few hundred dollars’ worth, consider moving it off the exchange into a wallet you control.

  • Send a small test transaction first.
  • Double-check the receiving address. Bitcoin transactions are not reversible.
  • Store your hardware wallet and seed phrase backup in safe places.
  • Make sure someone you trust could find it if something happened to you. Our estate planning workshops cover how.

Step 6: Hold and keep learning

Bitcoin is designed as a long-term store of value. For most people, the sensible approach is to buy, hold, and keep learning. Ignore daily price noise and focus on the big picture: fixed supply and a growing number of individuals and businesses holding bitcoin as a reserve asset.

Every sale, trade, or purchase made with bitcoin can be a taxable event in Canada. Read the Canadian Bitcoin tax overview before you sell or spend.

Key safety rules

  • Never share your seed phrase or private keys with anyone — ever.
  • Enable 2FA on every account (an authenticator app, not SMS, if you can).
  • Be extremely careful of scams: fake support, phishing emails, social media giveaways.
  • Double-check every address before sending.
  • Start small while you learn.

Quick start checklist

  1. Read this guide (you’re doing it!)
  2. Download the Shakepay app (or your chosen platform) and sign up
  3. Verify your identity
  4. Buy a small amount of bitcoin and save the transaction record
  5. Consider moving larger amounts to a hardware wallet
  6. Read the Canadian tax overview
