Why a business might hold Bitcoin as a reserve

We do not pitch bitcoin as a checkout button. Card and Interac already handle the till. The more useful question for an owner is what the business keeps on its balance sheet. The honest reasons people consider a bitcoin reserve are practical, not ideological:

  • A long-term savings slice. Some owners keep a defined, capped portion of surplus cash in bitcoin, separate from operating money. That is a policy choice, not a personality test.
  • Fixed supply, no counterparty. Bitcoin held in the company’s own custody is not a deposit someone else owes you. Understand that before you decide whether it belongs on your books.
  • A written “strategic Bitcoin reserve.” Larger companies publish treasury policies. A small business can do the same on one page: how much, where it is held, who signs, and when you rebalance.
  • Estate and continuity planning. A reserve the owner alone can access is a risk. Planning who can recover it is part of the job.

What it is not: a guarantee of growth, a way to skip taxes, or a replacement for an operating account and an emergency fund. Bitcoin is volatile. Only money the business can leave alone for years belongs in a reserve.

Where Shakepay for Business fits: treasury, not a till

We checked Shakepay’s own business page and help docs (as of August 2026). Shakepay for Business is not a point-of-sale system. It does not generate a CAD invoice QR at checkout, lock an exchange rate for this sale, or show staff a “paid” screen for that coffee.

What it actually is — and it is good at this:

  • A business treasury account for CAD, bitcoin, and ethereum
  • Buy and sell bitcoin with no trading commission (their current business pitch)
  • Move CAD with Interac e-Transfer and same-day wires to/from a bank account in the company’s name
  • Statements, recurring buys, and (if you opt in) interest on the CAD balance, paid in bitcoin

A customer can send bitcoin to your Shakepay business deposit address. That is a wallet transfer, not a till:

  • No locked CAD price for this sale
  • No automatic match to an invoice
  • On-chain payments can sit unconfirmed while a line forms
  • Easy to mix with other deposits and lose the audit trail

Do not print a static Shakepay QR on the counter and call it “we accept bitcoin.” Do not ask the customer to Interac you and pretend that was a bitcoin payment. Use a real POS for the sale. Use Shakepay for Business after the sale, for treasury and CAD.

Sizing a strategic Bitcoin reserve

Write this down before the business buys its first sat. Operations run in CAD. The reserve is a separate, capped decision. If the business also happens to receive bitcoin, the same choices apply:

  • Hold none (yet). Perfectly reasonable. Learn first, keep operations in CAD, and revisit once the owners understand custody. Any bitcoin the business does receive can go to Shakepay for Business, sell to CAD, Interac or wire to the company bank for rent and payroll.
  • Hold a small slice. Example: put a fixed, small share of surplus cash (never operating or tax money) into bitcoin under a written cap, bought on a schedule through a business account such as Shakepay for Business. Rebalance on a calendar, not a feeling.
  • Hold more. Only if the business can pay rent without that bitcoin. Move the reserve off the exchange onto a company hardware wallet with a two-person process if there is a team.

Keep the reserve separate from everyday money. The business owns the keys and keeps its own backups; never hand a seed phrase to an advisor, employee, or anyone else (including us). Record who can access the reserve and how it is recovered if an owner is unavailable — see estate planning.

Canadian tax, GST/HST, and bookkeeping notes

This is the part owners skip — and later regret. Take it to your accountant.

  • CRA generally treats bitcoin as a commodity, not as Canadian currency.
  • Buying and holding bitcoin in the company’s name is generally not a sale. Selling, trading, or spending it later can create a capital gain or loss (or business income) measured in CAD.
  • When you sell goods or services for bitcoin, you still have a sale. GST/HST is typically calculated on the CAD fair market value at the time of the sale.
  • The bitcoin you receive then has a cost base. If you later convert it to CAD at a different price, there may be a capital gain or loss (or business income, depending on how you operate).
  • Issue invoices in CAD (and show the bitcoin amount / rate / time if useful). Your accountant will thank you.
  • Payroll, trusts, and regulated industries have extra rules. Do not improvise those.

Record for every reserve purchase or sale: date/time, CAD amount, sats/BTC, rate, fees, and wallet. If you ever receive bitcoin for a sale, also record: date/time, CAD price charged, sats/BTC received, FX rate used, fees, invoice ID, and destination wallet. Keep those records at least six years.

For personal holdings vs business holdings, see the Canadian tax guide.

A one-page reserve policy

Print this, fill the blanks, and file it with your accountant package:

  1. Operations, payroll, and tax money stay in CAD. Minimum CAD buffer: $________.
  2. Reserve cap: $________ (or ____% of surplus cash), reviewed: ________.
  3. How we buy: ________ (e.g. a scheduled purchase through Shakepay for Business), on a calendar, not a feeling.
  4. Where it is held: ________ (company hardware wallet / business account). Withdrawal to self-custody: ________.
  5. Who can spend / who has the backup / how many signatures: ________. No one outside the company holds a seed phrase.
  6. If an owner is unavailable: ________ (continuity and estate plan reference).
  7. Bookkeeping export: weekly / monthly, stored with our accountant package.

First-month checklist

  1. Read this page with your bookkeeper.
  2. Decide: no reserve yet, or a written, capped slice. Open Shakepay for Business for the CAD on/off-ramp if you need one.
  3. Set up a company hardware wallet. Do not use the owner’s personal wallet as the company reserve.
  4. Make a small test purchase and a test withdrawal to that wallet. Export the records.
  5. Train one backup person. Write the “what if the owner is unavailable” step.
  6. Book a review with your accountant before the year-end.

If you are still at zero personally, learn that path first: Get off zero. A business that holds bitcoin should have at least one owner who understands custody.

If customers ask to pay in bitcoin

Accepting bitcoin at checkout is not our recommendation or our focus. If customers ask anyway, here is the background so you understand the trade-offs. Any bitcoin you receive follows the same reserve policy above.

How Bitcoin payments work

A customer pays an invoice denominated in CAD (or in sats). Software quotes a bitcoin amount at the current rate, usually with a short expiry. When the network confirms (or Lightning settles), you have been paid.

Two rails matter:

  • On-chain: the base Bitcoin network. Fine for larger invoices. Fees and confirmation time vary.
  • Lightning: faster, cheaper payments on top of Bitcoin. Better for coffee, tickets, and retail.

You do not need to explain any of that to a customer. They scan a QR code. You see “paid.”

If you do take it at the till

The till job is always the same, whatever software you pick:

  1. Ring the sale in Canadian dollars as you already do (tax included).
  2. Open the Bitcoin POS on a phone or tablet. Type that CAD total.
  3. Show the customer the dynamic QR (Lightning for small tickets, on-chain as a fallback).
  4. They pay from a Lightning/Bitcoin wallet (Phoenix, Strike, Wallet of Satoshi, and similar).
  5. Wait for the green Paid. Lightning is usually a few seconds. Then hand over the goods.
  6. Keep the invoice ID with the CAD amount, sats received, rate, time, and fees.

That is the whole customer experience. You should not explain channels, nodes, or mempools at the counter.

What not to do at the till

  • A printed, unchanging bitcoin address or Shaketag
  • A personal wallet on the owner’s phone as the “company till”
  • Telling the customer to “just send $20 of bitcoin” without a quoted amount and expiry
  • Handing bitcoin sales through a personal Shakepay account

Whatever you choose, confirm before go-live: CAD pricing, Lightning for retail tickets, a paid/unpaid screen staff cannot miss, and an export your bookkeeper can open.
